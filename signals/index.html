Methodology

How we test our predictions

1
Signal publishes a directional prediction
Each day at ~1pm UK (before US markets open at 2:30pm), our Narrative Asset Signal engine reads thousands of media, policy and supply chain sources and publishes a structured forecast: which asset, which direction (up or down), and how strong the pressure.
2
We map each prediction to a benchmark ETF
To test objectively, we pair each directional call with the most relevant exchange-traded fund. For example: an oil bullish signal maps to XLE (Energy Select Sector), an airlines bearish call maps to JETS (U.S. Global Jets), and a gold signal maps to GLD (SPDR Gold Shares). These are high-liquidity, widely-held ETFs that directly track the commodity in question.
3
We record the price at signal time, then track what happens
The moment a signal publishes, we record the ETF’s price as our entry point. We then sample every 30 minutes and formally score at four horizons: 6h, 12h, 24h, and 72h. If the ETF moved >0.5% in the predicted direction at any horizon, that’s a confirmed hit. If it’s moving in the right direction but hasn’t crossed the 0.5% threshold yet, it’s tracking. If it moved the wrong way, it’s a miss.
4
We measure how far ahead we were
The lead time is the gap between when we published the signal and when the market first moved >0.5% in that direction. This is the “edge window” — the period where we knew the likely direction before the market reflected it. Our headline stats only count direct proxies (ETFs with a clear, causal link to the signal). Secondary cascade effects (e.g., how an oil signal affects bond prices) are tracked separately under observation.
Direct Proxy
Clear causal link. Oil signal → energy ETF, airline signal → airline ETF. These are the fair tests and form our headline accuracy figure.
Indirect Proxy
Secondary effects with confounding factors. Bond prices, emerging market ETFs, and materials indices are monitored for learning but excluded from headline stats.
