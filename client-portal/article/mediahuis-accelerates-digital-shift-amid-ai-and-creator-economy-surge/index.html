Mediahuis accelerates digital shift amid AI and creator economy surge

By FTS Briefing Newsroom · 89 days ago

Mediahuis aims to shift 70% of revenue to digital by 2030 through the '7-7-7' plan The group is harnessing AI and M&A to address revenue challenges and engage younger audiences Industry trends show a rapid rise in creator economy mergers, shaping future media strategies

Mediahuis, a prominent European news publishing group operating across six countries, set an ambitious strategic goal in 2023 referred to as the "7-7-7" plan: to shift its revenue composition from 70% print-based to 70% digital-based within seven years. The group’s Chief Operating Officer, Paul Verwilt, openly acknowledged the challenge of operationalising this transformation given the evolving media landscape shaped by artificial intelligence (AI) and the rise of the creator economy. Forecasting models warn of a looming revenue gap exceeding €100 million by 2030 that Mediahuis will need to address to sustain its financial health and continue funding quality journalism.

Since its formation in 2014, Mediahuis has experienced significant revenue growth, from approximately €300 million to over €1.2 billion, achieved entirely through acquisition and scale without external investment. Despite this growth and high profitability, recent years have seen stagnation in both revenue and operating profit. This plateau has heightened concerns among the leadership team about the feasibility of reaching their long-term digital revenue target by 2030 and maintaining their journalism quality.

A notable success in this transition is Mediahuis’s progress in subscription revenue. The group has effectively increased average digital subscription prices from around six to eight euros to seventeen to eighteen euros, with data showing that customers often tolerate price increases with less churn than expected. However, digital advertising revenues are not meeting growth expectations, and print distribution costs, sometimes as high as 80 cents per copy, remain a costly burden.

Amid these challenges, AI and automation have taken centre stage at Mediahuis. AI technologies are integrated across four pillars: foundational organisation and infrastructure, transformation of newsroom workflows (with approximately 15 AI tools currently deployed), innovation in journalism products and audience engagement, and exploring new business opportunities in the digital age. Verwilt asserted that AI helps staff by automating repetitive tasks, enabling journalists to focus more on quality content creation and strategic innovation, while automation also assists in cutting print distribution costs.

One of the most pressing issues for Mediahuis is engaging younger audiences, as this demographic increasingly consumes content via creators and influencers outside traditional publisher platforms. Verwilt stressed the importance of either maintaining audiences within Mediahuis’s own platforms or creating value on external platforms to avoid collapse of their traditional business model. The company is experimenting with a multi-faceted approach including a Creator Economy Strategy guided by a Future Insights Board, talent development programmes, an active mergers and acquisitions strategy targeting adjacent creative industries, the building of dedicated communities focused on youth interests, and monetisation of content on third-party platforms like YouTube.

This focus on M&A aligns with wider industry trends. The maturation of the creator economy has triggered a surge in mergers and acquisitions, particularly accelerating in 2025 with a 73% year-on-year increase in deal activity. This surge has been driven by the integration of AI into marketing technology sectors, as traditional entertainment companies, private equity firms, and digital agencies consolidate to harness fragmented markets. Investors increasingly prioritise platforms that combine scalable AI tools with strong profitability and cross-industry appeal, a trend Mediahuis seems to be tapping into as it seeks to expand its digital and creative capabilities.

Collaboration within the industry is another strategic pillar for Mediahuis, with the group pursuing partnerships to address common operational costs such as printing and distribution. Verwilt highlighted the inefficiency of competitors duplicating efforts and stressed the value of shared resources and technological developments that can free up funds for unique journalism investments.

Ultimately, Mediahuis’s strategic evolution reflects the broader disruption and opportunity in the media sector, where AI-powered efficiencies and the burgeoning creator economy are reshaping how content is produced, distributed, and monetised. The success of the "7-7-7" vision will depend heavily on the group’s ability to innovate operationally, monetise new audience segments, and collaborate effectively at scale in an increasingly digital and competitive environment.
