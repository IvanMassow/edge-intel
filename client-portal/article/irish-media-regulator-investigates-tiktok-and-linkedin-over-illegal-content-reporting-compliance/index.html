Irish media regulator investigates TikTok and LinkedIn over illegal content reporting compliance

By FTS Briefing Newsroom · 88 days ago

Ireland's media regulator probes TikTok and LinkedIn over content reporting mechanisms Investigations could lead to fines of up to 6% of global turnover if violations are found Moves follow past fines and broader efforts to enforce EU digital safety standards

Ireland's media regulator, Coimisiún na Meán, has launched formal investigations into TikTok and LinkedIn over concerns that their mechanisms for reporting illegal content, particularly child sexual abuse material, may not fully comply with the European Union's Digital Services Act (DSA). The regulator is examining whether these platforms provide accessible, user-friendly systems for users to report such content, including the option for anonymous reporting, as mandated by the legislation. John Evans, the regulator's digital services commissioner, highlighted worries that the design of these platforms could deter users from effectively reporting illegal material. Should these investigations find the companies in violation, fines could reach up to 6% of their annual global turnover. This move follows a recent probe into the social media platform X under the same regulations, illustrating the regulator's continued scrutiny of online platforms' compliance with EU digital safety laws.

This investigation aligns with ongoing regulatory pressure on both companies regarding data protection and content oversight. TikTok, for example, remains under significant regulatory attention after the Irish Data Protection Commission fined the company €530 million in May 2025 for transferring the personal data of European Economic Area (EEA) users to China without adequate safeguards. The DPC found that TikTok violated the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) by permitting Chinese employees access to EEA user data stored outside China, a ruling TikTok plans to appeal. LinkedIn, meanwhile, was fined €310 million in October 2024 by the Irish Data Protection Commission for processing members' data for targeted advertising without valid consent in breach of GDPR requirements. LinkedIn also has appealed this decision, underlining its ongoing legal challenges related to regulatory compliance.

Beyond data protection, these investigations are part of broader efforts by Irish regulators to enforce European digital standards. In November 2024, Coimisiún na Meán ordered social media platforms including TikTok, Instagram, and X to enhance measures preventing the spread of terrorist content, demonstrating the regulator’s expanded remit under multiple EU digital laws such as the Terrorist Content Online Regulation and the Digital Services Act. Earlier in 2024, a formal review was launched into major platforms, TikTok, Meta, and X, to assess the ease with which users can report illegal content. This review was prompted by the fact that one-third of digital services complaints received by the regulator concerned difficulties in reporting such material.

The regulatory actions signal the increasing enforcement pressure on dominant online platforms to uphold user safety and privacy standards. Both TikTok and LinkedIn face serious consequences if found non-compliant, highlighting the EU's robust approach towards digital platform accountability. The investigations into reporting mechanisms reflect a crucial aspect of consumer protection online, aiming to ensure that harmful and illegal content can be flagged rapidly and anonymously where necessary, reinforcing safer digital environments for users across Europe.
