EU court clarifies online marketplaces' responsibilities under GDPR

By FTS Briefing Newsroom · 87 days ago

Court ruling defines when platforms are considered data controllers Operators shaping user content face new privacy obligations Changes may promote more privacy-respecting online commerce practices

Europe’s top court has clarified that operators of online marketplaces can be treated as data controllers under the EU’s General Data Protection Regulation when they exercise decisive influence over user-posted content, stripping them of a claim to mere neutrality. According to the original report, the Court of Justice found that platforms that control presentation, set usage rules and profit from visibility must answer for how personal data on their sites is processed. [1][4]

The judgment centred on the degree of control a platform exerts: where an operator shapes how listings appear, enforces rules and benefits commercially from prominence, it ceases to be a neutral host and becomes responsible for decisions about processing users’ information. Industry analysis and the court’s opinion emphasise that such factors determine controller status under the GDPR. [1][4]

The court highlighted that the post at the centre of the dispute disclosed sensitive personal details warranting “specific protection” because the context of processing could create significant risks to fundamental rights and freedoms. This reasoning echoes recent rulings and advocacy framing that challenge pervasive tracking and surveillance-based business models as incompatible with EU privacy law. [1][3]

As a consequence, operators may face direct GDPR obligations: verifying the identity of users who post adverts, taking steps to prevent unlawful publication of sensitive data, and otherwise ensuring the lawful basis and transparency of processing activities. Legal commentary notes these duties follow earlier ECJ findings that platform operators are controllers when they set up user accounts or influence processing. [5][4]

The decision comes amid wider regulatory momentum in the EU: courts have recently upheld stricter oversight of very large platforms under the Digital Services Act , a designation the General Court defended against Amazon’s challenge , signalling regulators’ willingness to treat large marketplaces as posing systemic risks that justify preventive obligations. The rulings together tighten the compliance landscape for marketplaces. [2][6][7]

Taken together, the rulings and related case law are likely to force marketplaces to redesign onboarding, moderation and monetisation practices to reduce privacy risks and align advertising and listing systems with GDPR principles , a shift advocates say would favour more rights‑respecting, less surveillance‑driven approaches to online commerce. [3][1][4]
