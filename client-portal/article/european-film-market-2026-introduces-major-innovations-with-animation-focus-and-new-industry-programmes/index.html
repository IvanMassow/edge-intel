European Film Market 2026 introduces major innovations with animation focus and new industry programmes

By FTS Briefing Newsroom · 88 days ago

Launch of EFM Animation Days to showcase Europe's animated works in progress Introduction of EFM Beyond for cross-media and immersive storytelling training New initiatives support emerging talent, international collaborations, and technological innovation

The European Film Market (EFM), held annually as part of the Berlinale, has announced an expanded and ambitious programme for its 2026 edition, scheduled to take place from February 12 to 18. The most notable addition is the launch of EFM Animation Days, a three-day event running from February 12 to 14, which signifies a major extension of the market’s activities. This new initiative aims to bring together animation creatives, studios, producers, buyers, financiers, and cross-IP partners through a curated array of showcases, pitching formats, case studies, workshops, and networking opportunities. The Annecy Animation Showcase EFM, curated jointly with MIFA and the Annecy International Animation Film Festival, will spotlight animated works in progress set for release in the latter half of 2026, targeting buyers and festival programmers. Supporting this new venture is the regional fund MDM Mitteldeutsche Medienförderung, underscoring the economic and cultural importance of Europe’s animation sector. Berlinale Pro director Tanja Meissner described the animation days as vital, recognising Europe’s strong global reputation for innovative and export-ready animation projects.

In addition, the EFM is introducing EFM Beyond, a forward-looking training programme focusing on innovative market strategies and cross-media collaboration. It aims to bridge film, animation, gaming, and adjacent creative industries, such as immersive storytelling in XR (extended reality). Primarily taking place in the EFM Producers Hub at the Gropius Bau, these sessions will include workshops on expanding intellectual property into new revenue streams and worldbuilding, alongside a cross-IP accelerator in collaboration with SpielFabrique and Annecy. This reflects a strategic shift toward embracing multi-platform storytelling and diversification within the creative economy.

Another new feature for the 2026 market is the Berlinale Film School Summit, which will welcome up to 100 selected directing and producing students from prominent international film schools. This programme offers early-career talents access to Berlinale Pro events, workshops, masterclasses, and invaluable networking opportunities, exemplifying the EFM’s commitment to nurturing emerging professionals.

The 2026 edition will also enhance industry support with new Work-in-Progress (WIP) and pitching sessions, notably the EFM Far East in Progress, a partnership with EAVE Ties that Bind and Focus Asia (Udine Far East Film Festival). This showcase presents Asian films in post-production that are poised to debut at autumn festivals and markets. Complementing this is the new EFM Frontières Focus programme, organised in collaboration with the Montreal-based Frontières market and Fantasia International Film Festival. This initiative highlights five international genre projects at advanced financing or early production stages, aiming to promote genre cinema globally by connecting projects with sales agents, distributors, and festival programmers.

The EFM Innovation Hub, spotlighting advances in production technology, will also return, alongside the 12th edition of the EFM Startups Programme. This entrepreneurial programme features ten media-tech startups offering innovative solutions across production, distribution, rights management, exhibition, and audience engagement. Meanwhile, following last year’s pilot, the EFM Investors Forum will resume, providing a platform for media finance leaders to engage in talks and networking.

Morocco has been chosen as the Country in Focus for 2026, a move designed to spotlight its blossoming production landscape and co-production potential, reflecting EFM’s ongoing efforts to diversify its geographical scope and foster international collaborations.

Moreover, the Berlinale Series Market (BSM) will return with fresh initiatives like Series Match, a collaboration with Iberseries & Platino Industria aimed at bridging Ibero-American and German series producers during early project stages. New features also include "Series Talks," a networking and showcase hub to be hosted at the CinemaxX lounge. Nonfiction professionals will find value in DocSalon, the Berlinale’s platform for documentary networking, which has expanded to include Archive Market for restored classics and heritage projects as well as a special focus on animated documentaries incorporated within the EFM Animation Days.

The EFM Conference Programme will run from February 13 to 16, offering a comprehensive schedule of talks, masterclasses, and panel discussions covering various industry topics. Attendees can tailor their experience through a broad range of sessions focused on producing, distribution, documentary, series, sustainability, diversity and inclusion, technology and innovation, animation, and archival content.

Tricia Tuttle, Berlinale’s festival director, highlighted the critical role of the European Film Market in supporting the film business ecosystem and ensuring cultural cinema reaches audiences worldwide. She applauded EFM’s innovative efforts towards future-proofing the market, embracing digital transformation, different film forms, and cultivating new talent pathways. These initiatives continue to attract tens of thousands of professionals from over 130 countries to Berlin every year, reaffirming EFM’s status as an essential hub for the global film industry.

The EFM Industry Programme is co-funded by Creative Europe MEDIA, with specialised support for different initiatives from regional bodies such as Hessen Film & Medien, Canada Media Fund, FFF Bayern, and Film- und Medienstiftung NRW. This extensive network of partners underlines the collaborative and pan-European nature of the market’s operations.

Overall, the 2026 expansion introduces significant innovations and broader inclusivity tailored to evolving market needs, signaling the European Film Market’s adaptive strategy to nurture creative growth and commercial success across a diverse range of film and media sectors.
