Retailers embrace entertainment to boost engagement and revenue strategies

By FTS Briefing Newsroom · 45 days ago

Bloomingdale’s launches a Wuthering Heights-themed pop-up linked to a film release Gap appoints a Chief Entertainment Officer to develop long-term media and licensing partnerships Both initiatives highlight a shift towards integrating cultural content to drive customer engagement and sales

Two high‑profile U.S. retailers are explicitly repositioning themselves at the intersection of commerce and entertainment, using film tie‑ins and senior hires to turn cultural content into marketing and product opportunities. Bloomingdale’s is staging a theatrical pop‑up tied to an upcoming movie release, while Gap Inc. has created a senior "chief entertainment" role to scale long‑term content and licensing partnerships. [1][2][3]

Bloomingdale’s, a unit of Macy’s Inc., has opened a Wuthering Heights‑themed Carousel Shop in its 59th Street flagship, running from January 15 to February 22. According to the company, the gothic, romantic installation , created in partnership with Warner Bros. Discovery , features exclusive product assortments including a 35‑piece AQUA x "Wuthering Heights" collection, immersive Valentine’s‑themed events and additional short‑run activations in SoHo, Aventura, Century City, Sherman Oaks and Roosevelt Field from January 15–22. The activation is timed to the film’s nationwide theatrical release on February 13, 2026, and culminates with multi‑city Galentine’s Day events on February 7; the studio’s campaign also includes original songs by Charli XCX. [2][1]

Gap Inc.’s move is structural: the company has appointed Pam Kaufman as its first Executive Vice President, Chief Entertainment Officer, a role effective February 2, 2026, that will report to president and CEO Richard Dickson and lead what Gap calls its "Fashiontainment" platform. The company said Kaufman will build and scale entertainment, content and licensing across music, television, film, sports, gaming, consumer products and cultural collaborations, operating from a soon‑to‑open Los Angeles office on Sunset Boulevard while spending substantial time in New York and San Francisco. Kaufman joins from Paramount, where she had been President and CEO of International Markets, Global Consumer Products and Experiences. [3][4][6]

Industry reporting places Kaufman’s hire in the context of wider talent movement after corporate reshuffles at major studios; several accounts note she left Paramount following the Skydance transaction last autumn. Gap’s announcement and subsequent press coverage frame the appointment as a deliberate bet that entertainment partnerships can drive brand relevance and create licensing and content revenue streams beyond traditional retail. The new Sunset Boulevard office, the company said, will serve as a hub for those ambitions and reflects a broader retail trend of embedding teams closer to creative hubs. [5][7][3]

Taken together, the two initiatives illustrate complementary short‑ and long‑term tactics brands are adopting to monetise cultural moments and to institutionalise content relationships. Bloomingdale’s pop‑up converts a single film release into product drops and experiential footfall; Gap is investing in a senior executive and a permanent West Coast presence to engineer ongoing collaborations across multiple media and IP owners. Retailers and analysts have increasingly pointed to such "entertainment" strategies as a way to deepen customer engagement, extend lifecycle value for product drops, and create licensing income streams amid a challenging apparel market. [1][2][3]
