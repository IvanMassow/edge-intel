JournalismAI Festival 2025 reveals global shifts in AI-driven journalism

By FTS Briefing Newsroom · 88 days ago

Highlights the global scope of AI applications in journalism from Latin America to Asia Examines the impact of AI on audience engagement, revenue, and content control Addresses challenges in verifying AI-generated content and combating deepfakes

Last month, the JournalismAI Festival 2025 convened in the Southwark area of South London, drawing journalists, newsroom leaders, AI strategists, and fact checkers from around the world to exchange insights on the evolving role of artificial intelligence in journalism. Hosted by the Google News Initiative and JournalismAI, a project of Polis at the London School of Economics, the event was the first in-person edition since its 2019 online inception. The festival offered a platform for examining both the practical applications of AI in newsrooms and the broader implications for the media industry and its audiences.

Attendees demonstrated a mature understanding of AI’s editorial benefits and constraints, signalling a shift from the initial hype surrounding generative AI technologies like ChatGPT. However, concern was palpable around AI’s downstream impact on audience engagement and the traditional news business model. Irene Jay Liu, director of AI, emerging tech, and regulation at the International Fund for Public Media, highlighted a stark decline in referral traffic in various markets such as Brazil, South Africa, and Indonesia, some publishers reporting drops of up to 60% in the past year. Liu attributed part of this decline to search engines summarizing journalistic content, a trend she cautioned could further destabilize already fragile news revenue streams.

Echoing this broader transformation, Ezra Eeman, strategy and innovation director at Dutch public broadcaster NPO, reframed the conversation from how newsrooms integrate AI internally to how media itself is becoming subsumed within AI systems. This paradigm shift suggests media content is being embedded as layers within AI-powered platforms, raising profound questions about control, distribution, and monetization.

Throughout the festival, the 35 recipients of JournalismAI’s 2024 Innovation Challenge grants, funded by the Google News Initiative, shared a variety of AI-driven projects, from AI-assisted article production and editing to distribution optimisation and organisational change management. The forthcoming Innovation Challenge, announced during the event, will pivot towards exploring AI’s role in audience intelligence and revenue growth, representing a strategic response to the pressing need for sustainable business models in journalism amid AI’s rise.

A notable strength of the festival was its international scope. Newsrooms from Latin America, Africa, and Asia showcased pioneering AI applications frequently overlooked in Western media discourse. At Indian digital news outlet Scroll, for example, an AI personalisation tool enables readers to tailor articles by sliding a bar to select varying levels of explanatory detail and length, focusing on delivery form rather than creating content silos. Similarly, Cuba’s El Toque leverages AI to analyse social media for real-time black market currency exchange data critical to small businesses, with plans to monetize this capability via subscription models. Tunisia’s Nawaat, facing state censorship, employs AI to maintain accessible archives spanning over two decades of independent reporting, including interactive timelines and thematic summaries, a digital living memory of the nation’s recent history.

The festival also underscored the complexities confronting newsrooms in verifying AI-generated content and combating deepfakes. Celine Samson of VERA Files in the Philippines, and Stephanie Burnett from Reuters, warned against reliance on single AI detection tools, advocating instead for a composite approach, likened to a "Swiss army knife", to address distinct types of deepfakes across audio and visual media. The video verification platform InVid, developed by Agence France–Presse in collaboration with European media outlets, was praised for its integrative functionality and collaborative approach with journalists, standing out amid a crowded market of commercially driven detection tools that often lag behind rapid AI model updates.

AI’s transformative potential was especially evident in investigative journalism. CalMatters demonstrated how its Digital Democracy website scrapes and consolidates extensive government datasets in California, automating the identification of story leads such as legislators’ voting behaviour and financial backers, intelligence that has yielded critically acclaimed investigations exerting real-world political influence. The project has recently expanded to cover Hawaii’s legislature in partnership with Honolulu Civil Beat. Similarly, Spain’s fact-checking organisation Newtral deploys an AI tool called FactFlow to monitor thousands of Telegram channels for disinformation narratives, dramatically reducing the time required to identify and prioritise content for deeper verification.

The JournalismAI Festival’s comprehensive sessions, recorded and made publicly available, reflected an industry-wide reckoning with AI’s dual-edged impact, its power to streamline journalistic workflows and enhance reporting capacity, balanced against concerns over audience fragmentation, misinformation, revenue erosion, and ethical imperatives. This multi-faceted picture points to a future where AI is not merely an editorial tool but an integral part of the media ecosystem, reshaping news production, distribution, and consumption globally.
